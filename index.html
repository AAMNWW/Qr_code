The Basics

What is a QR Code?

A barcode that went 2D and became a superstar.

A QR code — short for Quick Response code — is a two-dimensional barcode made of black squares arranged on a white square grid.

A smartphone camera can read it in under a second and turn it into useful information: a website link, a phone number, a Wi-Fi password, a payment or a secret message.

A normal barcode stores information in only one direction (left to right). A QR code stores it both across and down, so it can hold hundreds of times more data.

Normal Barcode (1D)
Reads one way only · about 20 characters
VS
QR Code (2D)
Reads both ways · up to 4,296 letters & numbers
💡
Did you know? It's called "Quick Response" because it was designed to be read very fast — about 10 times faster than other codes at the time.
History

From Car Factories to Every Pocket

How a tool for tracking car parts took over the world.

1994

Invented in Japan by engineer Masahiro Hara at Denso Wave, part of the Toyota group, to track car parts quickly in factories.

Free for All

Denso Wave chose not to charge anyone for using the QR code design, so anyone in the world could make and use them for free.

2000s

QR codes became an international standard (ISO/IEC 18004) and phones in Japan started coming with QR readers built in.

2017

iPhone and Android cameras began scanning QR codes automatically, with no extra app needed.

2020 – Today

The COVID-19 pandemic made QR codes part of daily life for contactless menus, payments and check-ins. Now they're everywhere!

How it Works

Inside a QR Code

Every QR code has special parts. Tap or hover over a part to see where it is.

👆 Pick a part to highlight it

Finder Patterns

The three big squares in the corners. They tell the scanner where the code is and which way up it is.

Alignment Pattern

A smaller square that helps read the code even when it's tilted or on a curved surface.

Timing Patterns

Dotted lines of black and white that help the scanner measure the size of the grid.

Quiet Zone

The empty white border that separates the code from everything around it.

Data Area

All the other squares, which hold the actual information.

Error Correction

Backup data mixed into the code so it still works if part of it is dirty or scratched.

1011001

Each tiny square is called a module. Black = 1, white = 0. This is binary, the language computers understand.

Error Correction Levels

QR codes have built-in backup data, so they can still be scanned when part of them is damaged. That's why some companies can put a logo right in the middle of a QR code and it still works!

Weighing it Up

The Good & The Not-So-Good

👍 Advantages

  • ✓ Super fast to scan — "Quick Response"
  • ✓ Stores far more data than a normal barcode
  • ✓ Free to create and use
  • ✓ Still works when partly damaged
  • ✓ Can be scanned from any angle
  • ✓ Contactless and saves paper

👎 Disadvantages

  • ✕ Needs a smartphone or scanner to read
  • ✕ Often needs internet to open links
  • ✕ You can't tell where it leads just by looking
  • ✕ Scammers can make fake QR codes
  • ✕ Won't scan if printed too small or blurry
Stay Safe

Watch Out for "Quishing"

Quishing (QR + phishing) is when scammers use fake QR codes to steal information. Here's how to protect yourself.

🔍
Check the link firstRead the web address your phone shows before you open it.
🏷️
Look for stickersScammers stick fake codes on top of real ones in public places.
🔐
Guard your detailsNever type passwords or bank details on a site from an unknown code.
👨‍👩‍👧
Ask an adultIf you're not sure a QR code is safe, ask before scanning.

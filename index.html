All About QR Codes

The little black-and-white squares that connect our world

What is a QR Code?

A QR code (short for Quick Response code) is a type of two-dimensional barcode. It is made of black squares arranged in a square grid on a white background. A smartphone camera or a scanner can read it in less than a second and turn it into useful information such as a website link, a phone number, a Wi-Fi password or a message.

A normal barcode (like the one on a chocolate bar) stores information in only one direction — left to right. A QR code stores information both horizontally and vertically, which is why it can hold much more data.

Did you know? A single QR code can store up to 7,089 numbers or 4,296 letters and numbers, while a normal barcode holds only about 20 characters!

History of the QR Code

1994

The QR code was invented in Japan by Masahiro Hara, an engineer at Denso Wave, a company that is part of the Toyota group. It was created to track car parts quickly in factories, because normal barcodes were too slow and held too little information.

Late 1990s

Denso Wave decided not to charge anyone for using the QR code design, so anyone in the world could create and use QR codes for free. This helped it spread quickly.

2000s

QR codes became an international standard (ISO/IEC 18004) and mobile phones in Japan started coming with QR code readers.

2017

Phone cameras on iPhone and Android started scanning QR codes automatically, with no extra app needed.

2020 – Today

During the COVID-19 pandemic, QR codes became part of everyday life for contactless menus, payments, and check-ins. Today they are used almost everywhere!

How Does a QR Code Work?

Every QR code is made of tiny squares called modules. A black module means 1 and a white module means 0 — this is called binary code, the language computers understand. The phone reads these patterns and converts them back into text or a link.

Each QR code has special parts that help the scanner read it:

🔲

Finder Patterns

The three big squares in the corners. They tell the scanner where the code is and which way up it is.

🎯

Alignment Pattern

A smaller square near the bottom-right corner that helps read the code even if it is tilted or curved.

📏

Timing Patterns

Lines of alternating black and white squares that help the scanner work out the size of the grid.

⬜

Quiet Zone

The empty white border around the code. It separates the QR code from everything around it.

💾

Data Area

All the remaining squares, which hold the actual information plus backup data.

🛠️

Error Correction

Extra backup data that lets the code still work even if part of it is dirty, scratched or covered.

Error Correction Levels

QR codes can still be read even when part of them is damaged. There are four levels:

LevelNameDamage it can survive
LLowAbout 7%
MMediumAbout 15%
QQuartileAbout 25%
HHighAbout 30%
Fun fact: Because of error correction, some companies put their logo in the middle of a QR code and it still scans perfectly!

🍽️

Restaurants

Digital menus and ordering food from your table.

💳

Payments

Paying in shops using mobile wallets and banking apps.

🎫

Tickets

Boarding passes, cinema tickets and event entry.

🏫

Linking worksheets to videos, quizzes and online lessons.

📦

Tracking parcels and products in warehouses.

📶

Connecting to Wi-Fi without typing a long password.

🏥

Healthcare

Patient wristbands and medicine information.

📢

Advertising

Posters and products that link to websites and offers.

Advantages & Disadvantages

✅ Advantages

  • Very fast to scan — "Quick Response"
  • Stores much more data than a normal barcode
  • Free to create and use
  • Works even when partly damaged
  • Can be scanned from any angle
  • Contactless and hygienic
  • Saves paper (e.g. digital menus)

❌ Disadvantages

  • You need a smartphone or scanner to read it
  • Often needs internet to open links
  • Humans can't tell where a code leads just by looking
  • Scammers can use fake QR codes
  • Does not work well if printed too small or blurry

Staying Safe with QR Codes

Some people create fake QR codes to trick others (this is called "quishing" — QR phishing). Follow these tips:

  • 🔍 Check the link your phone shows before opening it.
  • 🏷️ Look for stickers placed on top of real QR codes in public places.
  • 🔐 Never enter passwords or bank details on a site opened from an unknown QR code.
  • 📲 Don't download apps from QR codes unless you trust the source.
  • 👨‍👩‍👧 Ask an adult if you're not sure whether a QR code is safe.
